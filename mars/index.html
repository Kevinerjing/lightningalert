01 / 08🌱

Growing Potatoes

Potatoes are living stem tubers. Their 'eyes' can produce shoots. With light, water, carbon dioxide, nutrients and suitable temperature, plants grow through photosynthesis.

Martian regolith is not garden soil: it has little organic matter and may contain toxic perchlorates.

Plausible concept, major practical difficulties
02 / 08💧

Making Water

The film uses hydrazine rocket fuel as a source of hydrogen. Hydrogen then reacts with oxygen: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. This is both a synthesis reaction and a combustion reaction.

Forming water releases a great deal of heat; a premixed hydrogen–oxygen gas can explode.

Correct chemistry, hazardous execution
03 / 08🛰️

The Airlock Explosion

The habitat must be pressurized because Mars has less than 1% of Earth's surface air pressure. When the airlock breaks, gas rushes outward from high to low pressure.

The potato crop loses its protective environment, facing depressurization, cold and water loss.

Physically grounded, dramatized details
04 / 08🌪️

The Martian Dust Storm

Dust storms occur across Mars, and some can cover the entire planet. Yet the atmosphere is extremely thin, so even high-speed winds push much less strongly than similar winds on Earth.

Dust blocking sunlight and covering solar panels is a more serious danger than knocking over massive equipment.

Opening sequence greatly exaggerated
05 / 08☀️

Solar Power on Mars

Solar panels turn sunlight into electrical energy. On average, Mars gets about 43% of the sunlight per unit area that Earth gets, because Mars is farther from the Sun.

Dust accumulation and seasonal changes reduce the energy supply further.

Real mission engineering
06 / 08📡

Talking with Earth

Watney finds the old Pathfinder spacecraft and uses it to communicate. Radio waves move at the speed of light, but Earth–Mars signals can take several to more than twenty minutes one-way.

Long delays make real-time conversations impossible; messages must be sent asynchronously.

Based on real spacecraft communications
07 / 08🪐

The Gravity Assist

A spacecraft can pass near a moving planet and exchange orbital energy and momentum with it. Its speed and direction relative to the Sun can change without using as much fuel.

Gravity assists are used by real interplanetary spacecraft.

Real orbital mechanics
08 / 08🚀

The Final Rescue

When a jet of gas exits a spacesuit, momentum conservation pushes the astronaut the opposite way. This illustrates Newton's third law: forces occur in equal and opposite pairs.

A punctured spacesuit is a dangerous, imprecise way to steer in space.

Correct principle, unrealistic control
02 / THINK DEEPER

Three big questions.

Select a question to reveal its scientific explanation.

01Why can burning hydrogen make water and still cause an explosion? ＋

Hydrogen and oxygen react to create water: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Breaking old bonds absorbs energy, but forming strong O–H bonds releases even more. The net reaction releases heat. If a mixed gas ignites very rapidly, it heats and expands suddenly, causing a pressure wave. The final product can be ordinary water even though getting there is dangerous.

02Why did Watney's potatoes die after the airlock failed? ＋

Plants require more than just light. The airlock breach destroyed the habitat's controlled pressure and temperature. In the cold, low-pressure Martian environment, liquid water becomes unstable, plant cells lose water and may freeze, and tissues are damaged. Restoring the habitat later cannot necessarily bring severely damaged plants back to life.

03Why does a fast wind on Mars push less forcefully than the same wind on Earth? ＋

A useful model is dynamic pressure: q = ½ρv², where ρ is gas density and v is wind speed. Mars has an exceptionally low atmospheric density, so at the same speed its wind carries far less momentum per unit volume. Martian storms can still be serious because they block sunlight and cover equipment in dust.
