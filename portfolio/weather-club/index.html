Weather & Climate Club

Starting something new. Making atmospheric science accessible to more students.

01 / The beginning

Why I started the club

In Grade 9, I noticed that my school offered clubs focused on computer science, gaming and health, but none about weather or climate change. I've always been interested in meteorology, so I decided to start a club where students could explore these topics together.

I wanted the club to be welcoming even to people who had never studied atmospheric science.

02 / Activities

Learning by seeing and doing

WEATHER

Weather concepts

Introducing and discussing everyday weather and climate science.

DEMONSTRATION

Weather stations

Showing how weather-station equipment can be built and used.

ELECTRONICS

Lightning detection

Demonstrating how sensors can detect nearby lightning activity.

ENVIRONMENT

Air quality

Introducing monitoring equipment and observations of air quality at school.

03 / Reflection

Leadership is also listening.

At first, I thought that interesting technical projects would naturally attract students. When attendance declined after some meetings, I realized that I needed to think about what other students were comfortable learning.

I began introducing simpler weather and climate topics alongside demonstrations. The experience taught me that leading isn't just sharing my interests — it's making space for other people to explore them too.

“An idea becomes more meaningful when other people can take part in it.”
