Fahrenheit 451

Write one paragraph explaining what Ray Bradbury shows about a central theme. Try each question on your own, then open a hint if you get stuck.

Choose one theme

A topic is what the story is about. A theme is an idea the story shows about that topic. For example, “friendship” is a topic. “Friendship can help people face change” is a theme.

Thinking for yourself · Show hints· Hide hints

This is a good place to start. Think about Clarisse asking Montag whether he is happy and Montag later seeking help from Faber. What changes between someone asking Montag a hard question and Montag asking questions of his own?

Using entertainment to escape · Show hints· Hide hints

Think about Mildred calling the people on the TV walls her family. Compare her interest in the shows with how she responds when Montag wants to talk. What does that contrast suggest about what the shows give her and what she misses?

Censorship and fitting in · Show hints· Hide hints

Censorship means limiting what people can read, see, or say. Think about why Beatty says people turned away from books. Then think about the woman who stays with her books. What does her choice suggest about what people lose when books disappear?

Plan your paragraph

Claim → Context 1 → Evidence 1 → Explanation 1 → Context 2 → Evidence 2 → Explanation 2 → Closing sentence

  1. Claim. State what Bradbury shows about your theme. Introduce the title, year, and author.
  2. Context. Set up the quote. Who is speaking? Who are they talking to? What is happening right then?
  3. Evidence. Use a short quote from that scene and add the page number in parentheses. Use your own copy of the book to check the wording and page.
  4. Explanation. Pick a detail in the quote and explain what it shows. Then connect it to your claim.
  5. Repeat context, evidence, and explanation. Choose a second quote that adds something new to your point.
  6. Closing sentence. Return to your main idea and say what the two moments show together.

Claim. In Fahrenheit 451 (1953), Ray Bradbury shows that when people stop questioning what they are told, they can lose the ability to think for themselves.

Example paragraph

Try your own plan first. Then open the example to see what each sentence does.

Show example and sentence labelsHide example and sentence labels

In Fahrenheit 451 (1953), Ray Bradbury shows how constant entertainment can make it easier for people to ignore problems in their lives. When Montag wants Mildred to turn off the TV walls, she explains why she wants to keep watching. She tells him, “That’s my family” (XX). Calling the people on the screens her family shows how attached she is to the shows. In another conversation, Montag asks Mildred where they first met. As Mildred tries to remember, she says, “Funny, how funny, not to remember where or when you met your husband or wife” (40). Mildred calls the people on TV her family, yet she struggles to remember the start of her own marriage. Through Mildred, Bradbury shows how entertainment can give someone a feeling of connection while making it easier to ignore problems at home.

  1. Claim
    In Fahrenheit 451 (1953), Ray Bradbury shows how constant entertainment can make it easier for people to ignore problems in their lives.
    State the idea both scenes will support.
  2. Context 1
    When Montag wants Mildred to turn off the TV walls, she explains why she wants to keep watching.
    Set up who is speaking and what she is responding to.
  3. Evidence 1
    She tells him, “That’s my family” (XX).
    Replace XX with the page number from your copy.
  4. Explanation 1
    Calling the people on the screens her family shows how attached she is to the shows.
    Explain what Mildred’s choice of word reveals.
  5. Context 2
    In another conversation, Montag asks Mildred where they first met.
    Set up the scene before bringing in Mildred’s words.
  6. Evidence 2
    As Mildred tries to remember, she says, “Funny, how funny, not to remember where or when you met your husband or wife” (40).
    The example uses the page number in your draft. Check it in your copy.
  7. Explanation 2
    Mildred calls the people on TV her family, yet she struggles to remember the start of her own marriage.
    Put the two details together and explain the contrast.
  8. Closing sentence
    Through Mildred, Bradbury shows how entertainment can give someone a feeling of connection while making it easier to ignore problems at home.
    Return to the claim about ignoring problems.
