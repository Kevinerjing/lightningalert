Fahrenheit 451

Write one paragraph explaining what Ray Bradbury shows about a central theme. Try each question on your own, then open a hint if you get stuck.

Choose one theme

A topic is what the story is about. A theme is an idea the story shows about that topic. For example, “friendship” is a topic. “Friendship can help people face change” is a theme.

Thinking for yourself · Show hints· Hide hints

This is a good place to start. Think about Clarisse asking Montag whether he is happy and Montag later seeking help from Faber. What changes between someone asking Montag a hard question and Montag asking questions of his own?

Using entertainment to escape · Show hints· Hide hints

Think about Mildred calling the people on the TV walls her family. Compare her interest in the shows with how she responds when Montag wants to talk. What does that contrast suggest about what the shows give her and what she misses?

Censorship and fitting in · Show hints· Hide hints

Censorship means limiting what people can read, see, or say. Think about why Beatty says people turned away from books. Then think about the woman who stays with her books. What does her choice suggest about what people lose when books disappear?

Plan your paragraph

  1. Make a claim. What is Bradbury showing about your theme? Put your idea into one full sentence.
  2. Give your first piece of evidence. Pick a specific moment or a short quote. Give the reader just enough context to follow it.
  3. Explain why it matters. What does a detail in this scene reveal? How does it support your claim?
  4. Give your second piece of evidence. Pick a moment that adds something new, such as a change in a character or a contrast between two choices.
  5. Connect the two moments. What do they show together? Explain how that pattern supports your claim.
  6. End with the bigger idea. What does Bradbury leave readers thinking about?

Example paragraph

Try your own plan first. Then open the example to see what each sentence does.

Show example and sentence labelsHide example and sentence labels

In Fahrenheit 451, Ray Bradbury shows how constant entertainment can make it easier for people to ignore problems in their lives. Mildred calls the people on the TV walls her “family.” That word usually suggests a close relationship, but Mildred uses it for people on a screen. When Montag asks where they first met, neither of them can remember. Bradbury uses this contrast to show that Mildred feels close to the people on TV while she and Montag barely talk about their own lives. The shows give her a sense of connection without asking her to deal with the distance between her and her husband.

  1. Claim
    In Fahrenheit 451, Ray Bradbury shows how constant entertainment can make it easier for people to ignore problems in their lives.
    This gives the main idea the rest of the paragraph will support.
  2. Evidence 1
    Mildred calls the people on the TV walls her “family.”
    This points to a specific word Mildred uses in the novel.
  3. Analysis 1
    That word usually suggests a close relationship, but Mildred uses it for people on a screen.
    This explains why the word “family” matters.
  4. Evidence 2
    When Montag asks where they first met, neither of them can remember.
    This gives a second moment from the novel.
  5. Analysis 2
    Bradbury uses this contrast to show that Mildred feels close to the people on TV while she and Montag barely talk about their own lives.
    This connects both scenes and explains what they show about Mildred’s real relationships.
  6. Closing sentence
    The shows give her a sense of connection without asking her to deal with the distance between her and her husband.
    This returns to the main idea by explaining what the entertainment helps Mildred avoid.
