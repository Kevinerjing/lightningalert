Unit 1 · Business Fundamentals

Explain revenue, costs and profit.

Revenue is money earned from sales. Costs include the amounts spent to produce and run the business. Profit = revenue − all costs. Count each listed cost/expense once.

Revenue is $15,000 and costs are $9,500. Calculate profit.

$15,000 − $9,500 = $5,500 profit. Show the formula and units.

Define sole proprietorship.

An unincorporated business owned by one person, with no separate legal identity from the owner and unlimited liability.

Define partnership.

Two or more owners share business resources, responsibilities and profit under an agreement. General partners have unlimited liability; limited partnerships distinguish general and limited partners.

Define corporation.

A separate legal entity with ownership divided into shares. Shareholders generally have limited liability but can lose their investment.

Define co-operative.

A member-owned organization serving members’ shared needs. Member decisions commonly use one member, one vote.

Define franchise.

An arrangement allowing a franchisee to operate using a franchisor’s brand and business system in return for fees and following its rules.

Contrast limited and unlimited liability.

Unlimited liability can expose an owner’s personal assets to business debts. Limited liability generally limits shareholders’ exposure to their investment; it does not guarantee the business will succeed.

Explain all six consumption influences, giving an example for each.

Income & price: a budget changes what is affordable. Status: buying to impress. Trends: choosing a popular style. Safety: choosing protective equipment. Advertising: a promotion attracts interest. Situational factors: time, surroundings, purchase terms, purpose, and mood can change a decision.

Unit 2 · Check the wording

True or false: the law of demand describes an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

True, ceteris paribus. A higher own price means lower quantity demanded along the same demand curve.

True or false: the law of demand says price and quantity supplied move in opposite directions.

False. Demand concerns quantity demanded. The law of supply generally describes a positive relationship between price and quantity supplied.

Nike basketball shoes · Supply or demand?

For each prompt, identify the market, determinant, affected curve and direction. These are shifts at every price, not changes caused by Nike’s own selling price.

1. A famous athlete wears Nike basketball shoes.

Demand increases → right. Tastes/preferences: greater popularity can increase Nike demand at every price.

2. Buyers expect Nike basketball-shoe prices to rise next month. Analyse now.

Current demand increases → right. Expectations: buyers may purchase before the expected price increase.

3. Rubber and other production materials become more expensive.

Supply decreases → left. Resource costs increase (STERN). More costly production reduces supply at each price.

4. A new athletic-footwear company enters Canada.

Scope-sensitive: whole basketball-shoe supply increases; Nike demand may decrease. For the broad basketball-shoe market, more sellers means supply right. For Nike specifically, an attractive competing substitute may reduce Nike demand (left). Entry does not automatically increase Nike’s own supply. State the market and assumption; the worksheet’s all-Nike heading makes a single answer ambiguous.

5. The government increases the tax on producing athletic footwear.

Supply decreases → left. Production taxes increase costs (STERN). This is not an own selling-price movement.

6. A major Nike factory shuts down unexpectedly.

Supply decreases → left. Nike production capacity falls.

7. A new machine produces basketball shoes faster.

Supply increases → right. Improved production technology increases efficiency (STERN).

8. Running-shoe prices fall dramatically.

Nike basketball-shoe demand decreases → left, if the shoes are substitutes. Related goods: buyers switch toward the cheaper alternative. State the substitute assumption; different shoe uses can affect substitutability.

9. Basketballs, treated as complements, become more expensive.

Nike basketball-shoe demand decreases → left. Related goods: a higher complement price reduces demand for the associated good, under the worksheet’s stated complement assumption.

Unit 3 · CSR & International Business

What does CSR stand for and mean? Name its three components in this course.

Corporate Social Responsibility: responsible operations and contribution to society. The components here are sustainability (future generations), transparency (openness and accessible information), and social audits (unbiased third-party assessments). Do not confuse these components with the triple bottom line.

Name the triple bottom line.

Profit, people, planet: financial performance, wellbeing of people, and environmental responsibility.

Why can CSR be good for business?

It can build consumer loyalty, attract socially responsible investors, retain staff, improve workplace safety and increase efficiency. Give an example and acknowledge possible short-term costs.

Give two international-business benefits for businesses and two for consumers.

Businesses: larger markets and possibly lower costs. Consumers: specialized quality and increased variety. Benefits do not eliminate shipping, adaptation or other costs.

True or false: an import is something a country sells abroad.

False. Imports come into a country; exports are sold abroad. State the country’s perspective.

True or false: a trade surplus means imports exceed exports.

False. A surplus means exports exceed imports; imports exceeding exports means a deficit.

Describe one ethical issue in international business.

Possible answer: outsourcing to a supplier with unsafe conditions or child labour can harm workers. Another: foreign demand can cause resource use faster than replenishment, leading to environmental degradation. Explain the impact, not just the label.

In the teacher’s model, is North America strongly future-oriented?

True in the class comparison. Discuss plans and goals while recognizing cultural comparisons do not describe every individual.

Why understand greetings, time orientation and nonverbal communication?

To avoid misunderstanding, show respect, build trust and adapt communication. For example, silence or nodding should not automatically be interpreted as understanding or agreement.

Self-check before the test

For a definition: term + meaning. For a scenario: evidence + concept + consequence. For a curve change: market + determinant + curve + direction; only predict equilibrium effects when the other curve is held constant, or explain ambiguity when both change.
