Open answer key

Part A · Multiple choice (12 marks)

Choose the single best answer. One mark each.

1. A business earns $950 in sales and has total costs and expenses of $1,020. Which statement is correct?

  1. Revenue is −$70.
  2. It has a $70 loss.
  3. It has a $950 profit.
  4. Its expenses must be zero.

2. A store buys finished backpacks and resells them without changing them. Its main business type is:

  1. Manufacturing
  2. Public sector
  3. Merchandising
  4. Co-operative

3. A business has exactly 100 employees. Using the class categories, it is:

  1. Small
  2. Medium
  3. Large
  4. Impossible to classify without revenue

4. Which organization is clearly in the public sector?

  1. A municipal government-operated library
  2. A publicly traded clothing company
  3. A privately owned café open to everyone
  4. A family-owned franchise

5. Which statement best describes a sole proprietorship?

  1. Any business with only one worker
  2. A separate legal entity owned by shareholders
  3. An arrangement with no personal debt risk
  4. A business with one owner who generally has unlimited liability

6. Which feature best identifies a co-operative?

  1. All profits go to one owner.
  2. Members normally get one vote each in member decisions.
  3. More membership shares always give more votes.
  4. Every location pays a brand owner royalties.

7. An owner wants established branding, training and shared advertising, and accepts fees and operating rules. Which arrangement best fits?

  1. Franchise
  2. Non-profit
  3. Public sector department
  4. Independent sole proprietorship only

8. A student buys a luxury watch mainly to gain admiration. Which Maslow level is the strongest match?

  1. Physiological
  2. Safety
  3. Self-actualization
  4. Esteem

9. Which is a criticism of Maslow’s theory discussed in class?

  1. It recognizes only two needs.
  2. It says no one buys food.
  3. People’s priorities do not always follow the same fixed order.
  4. It defines business size by revenue.

10. A customer buys extra snacks because they shop while hungry. Which situational subtype is most directly shown?

  1. Terms and conditions
  2. States and moods
  3. Surroundings
  4. Objective: a special gift

11. The government reduces personal income taxes, leaving households more after-tax money. Which article factor is most directly illustrated?

  1. Commuting
  2. Seasonality
  3. New products
  4. Fiscal policy

12. Fuel prices rise. Why might a lower-income household reduce restaurant visits more than a higher-income household?

  1. Fuel costs can use a larger share of its available budget.
  2. Private restaurants are unaffected by fuel prices.
  3. Everyone reduces every purchase by the same amount.
  4. Restaurant meals are always survival needs.

Part B · Short answers (24 marks)

Four marks per question. Use precise terms and complete explanations.

13. Business and non-profit purpose (4 marks)

(a) Define a business and state its usual purpose. (2)
(b) A community non-profit collects more money than it spends. Explain why this does not automatically make it a for-profit business. (2)

14. Profit calculation (4 marks)

A stall sells 80 meals at $12 each. Ingredients cost $320, packaging $40, daily rent $90, wages $180 and the daily permit $30. Calculate revenue, total costs and expenses, and profit or loss. Show your formula. (4)

15. Business activities versus ownership (4 marks)

A family-owned company builds tables from wood, resells finished lamps and repairs customers’ chairs. It is not government-owned.
(a) Classify the three activities. (3)
(b) State its sector. (1)

16. Choosing an ownership form (4 marks)

Two friends want to combine their different skills, invest together and share day-to-day work.
(a) Explain why a partnership could fit them, using two scenario details. (2)
(b) Explain unlimited liability in a general partnership. (1)
(c) Give one reason they might instead choose a corporation. (1)

17. Needs and Maslow (4 marks)

(a) Distinguish a need from a want using basic winter clothing and a luxury-branded winter coat. (2)
(b) Someone buys an art course to reach their personal creative potential. Identify the Maslow level and explain why. (2)

18. Situational factors and advertising (4 marks)

Identify the most specific influence in each case. For the first three, name the situational subtype.
(a) A store’s pleasant lighting and music make a customer stay longer. (1)
(b) A customer chooses a store because it offers instalment payments. (1)
(c) A student buys a haircut specifically for graduation photographs. (1)
(d) A customer tries a drink after an influencer’s sponsored recommendation. (1)

Part C · Case analysis (24 marks)

Use details in the cases. Reasoned alternatives may earn credit where a recommendation is requested.

19. Rally Café (12 marks)

Rally Café operates as a corporation under a franchise agreement. The franchisor supplies training, an established brand and shared advertising. Rally must follow the approved menu and supplier rules. For a tournament weekend, its forecast sales revenue is $4,000. Forecast ingredients cost $1,400; wages, rent and other operating expenses total $1,600. The agreement requires a royalty of 5% of sales revenue, not yet included in those costs.

(a) Distinguish the franchisor’s role from the franchisee’s role. (2)
(b) Explain two benefits and one drawback of the franchise arrangement for Rally. (3)
(c) Explain what limited liability means for an ordinary shareholder, including what they could still lose. Assume no personal guarantees or other exceptions. (2)
(d) Calculate forecast profit after the royalty. Show your steps. (3)
(e) One owner says: “Because we are a franchise, our legal ownership form cannot be a corporation.” Correct this statement. (2)

20. Why spending changes (12 marks)

A town’s downtown businesses observe the following:

  • A: Holiday gift purchases rise every December.
  • B: A newly released gaming device attracts long queues.
  • C: More staff return to downtown offices three days a week.
  • D: Investments fall in value, making some households feel less wealthy.
  • E: The town has fewer families with young children than five years ago.
  • F: Jobs are secure, but food and fuel prices are rising faster than wages.

A major sports tournament is also bringing visiting families to town this weekend.

(a) Match A–E to five distinct factors from the article’s list of eight. (5)
(b) Using F, explain how secure employment and prices rising faster than wages can push spending in different directions. (2)
(c) Explain one way higher fuel prices could affect a lower-income household differently from a higher-income household. (2)
(d) Explain how the tournament could affect a restaurant near the venue and recommend one practical response. Include the causal link. (3)

End of test · Total: 60 marks

Check: all expenses included? Labels correct? “Because” explains a real link? Correct number of points?

Finished? Show answers and marking guide

Part A · Multiple choice — 12 marks

One mark per correct answer; no deductions for incorrect answers.

  1. B — Profit = $950 − $1,020 = −$70. Sales revenue is still $950.
  2. C — Buying and reselling finished products is merchandising.
  3. B — Medium-sized businesses have 100–499 employees.
  4. A — Government ownership/operation determines public sector, not public access or public share sales.
  5. D — The owner and business are not legally separate. It can employ workers.
  6. B — Member governance uses one member, one vote, not one vote per membership share.
  7. A — These are key benefits and restrictions of franchising.
  8. D — The motive is recognition/status; luxury objects do not automatically indicate self-actualization.
  9. C — The order may differ across people and cultures.
  10. B — Hunger is a temporary physical state that can affect purchasing.
  11. D — Tax changes are fiscal policy. More available money may be spent or saved.
  12. A — Budget constraints and travel costs can produce different responses across income groups.

Part B · Short answers — 24 marks

13. Business and non-profit purpose

Business: produces/sells goods or services to meet market demand (1), usually aiming to earn profit for owners (1). Non-profit: its main aim is a mission rather than owner profit (1); a surplus can be retained/used to support that mission (1).

14. Profit calculation

Revenue = 80 × $12 = $960 (1). Total costs/expenses = $320 + $40 + $90 + $180 + $30 = $660 (1). Correct profit formula/substitution, $960 − $660 (1). $300 profit, correctly labelled (1).

15. Business activities versus ownership

Tables: manufacturing (1). Lamps: merchandising (1). Repairs: service (1). Private sector (1). “Family-owned” alone does not identify its legal ownership form; do not infer sole proprietorship or partnership.

16. Choosing an ownership form

Partnership pools their different skills (1) and shares investment or daily responsibilities (1). General partners can be personally responsible for business debts; personal assets may be at risk (1). Corporation: generally limited shareholder liability, raising equity through shares, or continuity; any one explained feature (1).

17. Needs and Maslow

Need: adequate warm clothing protects basic survival in cold weather (1). Want: the luxury brand adds preference/status/pleasure beyond meeting that basic need (1). Self-actualization (1), because the motive is developing potential/personal growth rather than impressing others (1).

18. Situational factors and advertising

(a) Physical/social surroundings (1). (b) Terms and conditions of purchase (1). (c) Objective/purpose of purchase (1). (d) Promotion and advertising (1). These answers identify the strongest stated clues.

Part C · Case analysis — 24 marks

19. Rally Café — 12 marks

(a) 2: Franchisor licenses the brand/system and provides support (1); franchisee operates under that system, paying fees and following the agreement (1).

(b) 3: Any two explained benefits (1 each): established brand helps attract customers; training helps operations; shared advertising supports promotion. One drawback (1): fees reduce money retained, or menu/supplier rules reduce control. Award each distinct idea once.

(c) 2: The corporation is separate, so ordinary shareholders are generally not personally responsible for its business debts (1). Their investment/share value can still be lost (1).

(d) 3: Royalty = 0.05 × $4,000 = $200 (1). Profit = $4,000 − ($1,400 + $1,600 + $200) (1) = $800 (1). Do not calculate 5% of profit.

(e) 2: Franchising describes a licensing/operating arrangement (1). A corporation is a legal ownership structure, so Rally can be both a franchisee and a corporation (1).

20. Why spending changes — 12 marks

(a) 5: A seasonality; B new products; C commuting; D stock market performance; E demographic changes. One mark each. In D, use the most specific article factor rather than the broader macroeconomic category.

(b) 2: Secure jobs improve confidence in future income and may encourage purchases (1). Prices growing faster than wages reduce purchasing power and may force cuts or cheaper choices (1). More dollars spent does not necessarily mean more items bought.

(c) 2: A lower-income household may have less budget flexibility, so extra fuel costs can take money away from optional purchases (1). A higher-income household may absorb the increase with less change in driving or restaurant visits (1). Accept other well-explained contrasts; avoid assuming every household behaves identically.

(d) 3: Visiting families increase the potential customer base near the venue (1); they need meals/convenient food, which can raise restaurant demand (1). A linked response, such as adding peak-hour staff, stocking suitable meals or adjusting opening times (1).
