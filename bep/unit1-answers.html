Mock Test 1 · Answer key

Revised 37-question version · Matched to teacher Kahoot topics

One mark per correct answer. No penalty for an incorrect answer. Total: 37 marks.

1. Which term describes producing or selling goods or services to meet marketplace demand?

A. Business

A business supplies goods or services to meet demand, generally with a profit motive.

2. What is the usual main purpose of a for-profit business?

C. To earn profit for its owners or shareholders

Meeting customer demand is how the business aims to earn profit for its owners.

3. A snack stall expects $250 in sales revenue. Ingredients cost $65 and packaging costs $25. There are no other costs. What is its expected profit?

B. $160

Profit = revenue − all costs = $250 − ($65 + $25) = $160.

4. An organization raises money to provide emergency shelter. Its primary goal is its community mission rather than profit for owners. It is best described as a:

D. Non-profit

A non-profit prioritizes its mission. It can still receive revenue and retain a surplus for its work.

5. Most Canadian businesses are large businesses employing 500 or more people.

B. False

False. Most are small or medium-sized. In class: small = 1–99 employees; medium = 100–499; large = 500+.

6. A customer pays a barber only for cutting their hair. What are they buying?

A. A service

The haircut is work performed for the customer, so it is a service.

7. A customer receives a prepared meal and table service at a sit-down restaurant. The purchase includes:

C. A good and a service

The food is a tangible good; serving the customer is a service. This matches the restaurant distinction in the class practice.

8. A mechanic charges only for labour to adjust an existing car part. No new parts are supplied. This charge is for:

B. A service

The labour is a service. If a question also included newly supplied parts, the purchase could include goods as well.

9. A pair of running shoes bought from a shop is:

D. A good

The shoes themselves are a tangible item: a good.

10. A shop buys finished water bottles from a supplier and resells them unchanged. Its main activity is:

C. Merchandising

Merchandising means buying and reselling finished goods.

11. A company turns fabric and zippers into backpacks. Its main activity is:

A. Manufacturing

Transforming raw materials or parts into a product is manufacturing.

12. A business earns money by walking customers’ dogs. Which business type best fits?

B. Service

It performs an activity for customers. Corporation describes ownership, not the activity supplied.

13. A recreation centre owned and operated by a municipal government belongs to the:

D. Public sector

Government ownership/operation identifies the public sector. Being open to the public alone does not.

14. The class materials describe profit and competition as forces that generally encourage private-sector efficiency and innovation.

A. True

True in the classroom comparison. This is a generalization, not a claim that every private organization is more efficient or profitable than every public one.

15. The legal responsibility to pay debts is called:

C. Liability

Liability means responsibility for debts and obligations in this lesson.

16. For this lesson, when owners’ personal assets are generally separated from the business’s debts, the owners have:

B. Limited liability

Limited liability generally protects owners from personal responsibility for business debts. They can still lose their investment.

17. Two accountants jointly own a business, combine their client bases and share costs under an agreement. Which form is described?

D. Partnership

A partnership has two or more owners who share resources and responsibilities.

18. In a limited partnership, which type of partner has unlimited liability?

A. General partner

The general partner has unlimited liability. Do not confuse the partnership’s name with every partner’s level of liability.

19. A limited partnership must include at least one:

C. General partner

A limited partnership includes at least one general partner; it also has at least one limited partner. The general partner is the answer to the classroom distinction.

20. Residents jointly own a grocery business to meet members’ needs. Each member has one vote in member decisions. This is a:

B. Co-operative

Member ownership and one member, one vote are core co-operative features. Financial contributions need not be identical.

21. Which ownership form is generally the simplest to establish and administer?

D. Sole proprietorship

A sole proprietorship is the basic one-owner form. Simplicity does not remove the owner’s responsibilities.

22. A sole proprietor is generally personally responsible for the business’s debts and legal obligations.

A. True

True. The business and owner are not separate legal entities; liability is unlimited.

23. Which form has a separate legal identity and ownership divided into shares?

C. Corporation

A corporation is a legal entity separate from its shareholders.

24. Using the classroom description, a corporation that does not trade its shares on the stock market and is described as limited to 50 shareholders is a:

B. Private corporation

Private corporation is the expected class answer. The “50 shareholders” wording is the class simplification, not a universal definition for every legal context.

25. A government-owned corporation is called a:

D. Crown corporation

Crown corporation means government-owned. Publicly traded does not mean government-owned.

26. According to the lesson, why might a government privatize a Crown corporation?

A. To try to improve operating efficiency

Improving efficiency is a stated aim. Privatization means a transfer to private ownership; the desired result is not guaranteed.

27. An operator pays to use another company’s brand and business system, receives marketing support and follows its rules. The arrangement is a:

C. Franchise

The franchisee pays for access to the franchisor’s brand/system and support, in exchange for fees and operating restrictions.

28. Items that add comfort or pleasure beyond basic survival requirements are called:

B. Wants

Wants add comfort or pleasure. Needs are necessary for survival in the class definition.

29. A student buys expensive designer shoes mainly to impress others. Which Maslow level best fits the stated motive?

D. Esteem

Admiration, recognition and status point to esteem. The reason for the purchase matters.

30. A household buys a doorbell camera to feel protected. Which Maslow level is most directly involved?

A. Safety

Protection and security relate to safety needs.

31. Someone buys an online course to develop their abilities and reach their personal potential. Which Maslow level fits best?

C. Self-actualization

Personal growth and realizing potential are self-actualization. Buying a course purely for status could have a different motive.

32. Someone buys a phone mainly to stay connected with friends and family. Which Maslow level best fits?

B. Love and belonging

Connection and relationships are love and belonging needs.

33. Someone buys food and drinking water to meet basic survival needs. Which Maslow level applies?

D. Physiological

Food and water meet basic physiological needs.

34. Which statement is NOT one of the criticisms of Maslow’s theory listed in class?

A. People are motivated only by basic survival needs.

Choose the NOT option. The other three are criticisms taught in class; the theory itself includes motivations beyond survival.

35. Conspicuous consumption means displaying purchases to impress others. Which consumption influence does it belong to?

C. Status

The defining motive is displaying success or gaining admiration: status.

36. According to the class article, a strong job market and greater employment confidence generally encourage:

B. Higher consumer spending

Job security and income confidence can make consumers more willing and able to spend; the effect is a tendency, not a certainty.

37. Birth rates fall over many years. According to the article’s example, what is a likely effect on demand for baby and young-child products, other things equal?

D. It tends to decrease.

Fewer young children can mean fewer buyers for child-related products. This scopes the teacher’s broad question more precisely: it does not prove that total spending on every category must fall.
