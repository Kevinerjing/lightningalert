Part B · Short answers — 24 marks

13. Business and non-profit purpose

Business: produces/sells goods or services to meet market demand (1), usually aiming to earn profit for owners (1). Non-profit: its main aim is a mission rather than owner profit (1); a surplus can be retained/used to support that mission (1).

14. Profit calculation

Revenue = 80 × $12 = $960 (1). Total costs/expenses = $320 + $40 + $90 + $180 + $30 = $660 (1). Correct profit formula/substitution, $960 − $660 (1). $300 profit, correctly labelled (1).

15. Business activities versus ownership

Tables: manufacturing (1). Lamps: merchandising (1). Repairs: service (1). Private sector (1). “Family-owned” alone does not identify its legal ownership form; do not infer sole proprietorship or partnership.

16. Choosing an ownership form

Partnership pools their different skills (1) and shares investment or daily responsibilities (1). General partners can be personally responsible for business debts; personal assets may be at risk (1). Corporation: generally limited shareholder liability, raising equity through shares, or continuity; any one explained feature (1).

17. Needs and Maslow

Need: adequate warm clothing protects basic survival in cold weather (1). Want: the luxury brand adds preference/status/pleasure beyond meeting that basic need (1). Self-actualization (1), because the motive is developing potential/personal growth rather than impressing others (1).

18. Situational factors and advertising

(a) Physical/social surroundings (1). (b) Terms and conditions of purchase (1). (c) Objective/purpose of purchase (1). (d) Promotion and advertising (1). These answers identify the strongest stated clues.

Part C · Case analysis — 24 marks

19. Rally Café — 12 marks

(a) 2: Franchisor licenses the brand/system and provides support (1); franchisee operates under that system, paying fees and following the agreement (1).

(b) 3: Any two explained benefits (1 each): established brand helps attract customers; training helps operations; shared advertising supports promotion. One drawback (1): fees reduce money retained, or menu/supplier rules reduce control. Award each distinct idea once.

(c) 2: The corporation is separate, so ordinary shareholders are generally not personally responsible for its business debts (1). Their investment/share value can still be lost (1).

(d) 3: Royalty = 0.05 × $4,000 = $200 (1). Profit = $4,000 − ($1,400 + $1,600 + $200) (1) = $800 (1). Do not calculate 5% of profit.

(e) 2: Franchising describes a licensing/operating arrangement (1). A corporation is a legal ownership structure, so Rally can be both a franchisee and a corporation (1).

20. Why spending changes — 12 marks

(a) 5: A seasonality; B new products; C commuting; D stock market performance; E demographic changes. One mark each. In D, use the most specific article factor rather than the broader macroeconomic category.

(b) 2: Secure jobs improve confidence in future income and may encourage purchases (1). Prices growing faster than wages reduce purchasing power and may force cuts or cheaper choices (1). More dollars spent does not necessarily mean more items bought.

(c) 2: A lower-income household may have less budget flexibility, so extra fuel costs can take money away from optional purchases (1). A higher-income household may absorb the increase with less change in driving or restaurant visits (1). Accept other well-explained contrasts; avoid assuming every household behaves identically.

(d) 3: Visiting families increase the potential customer base near the venue (1); they need meals/convenient food, which can raise restaurant demand (1). A linked response, such as adding peak-hour staff, stocking suitable meals or adjusting opening times (1).
